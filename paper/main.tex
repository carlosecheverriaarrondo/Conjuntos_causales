\title{Boost-range scaling of the fluctuations of the Benincasa--Dowker action and its consequences for Everpresent $\Lambda$}

\author{Carlos Echeverr\'{\i}a-Arrondo}

\affil{Instituto de Dise\~no para la Fabricaci\'on y Producci\'on Automatizada, MALTA Consolider Team, Universitat Polit\`ecnica de Val\`encia, 46022, Val\`encia, Spain}

\email{cecharr@idf.upv.es}

\keywords{causal sets, Benincasa--Dowker action, cosmological constant, Everpresent Lambda, fluctuations}

\begin{abstract}
Everpresent $\Lambda$ is a causal-set inspired proposal in which the
cosmological constant fluctuates as $\delta\Lambda\propto V^{-1/2}$, with $V$
the spacetime volume of the observer's past, an exponent inherited from the
Poisson fluctuations of the number of elements. Moradi, Yazdi and Zilh\~ao
asked whether these fluctuations could instead be sourced by the
Benincasa--Dowker (BD) action, and gave a formalism for its variance without
evaluating it. We evaluate it analytically and with Poisson sprinklings in
four dimensions. The leading term, the Poisson noise in the number of nearly
null intervals, grows with the range of Lorentz boosts that fits inside the
region and scales exactly as $(\rhoV)^{2-2/d}$. In causal diamonds, past
light cones and past cones of flat FLRW spacetimes reaching the big bang, the
remaining terms of the local action are a constant fraction of it over the
simulated range ($\rhoV\le25\,600$), so that $\Delta S\propto(\rhoV)^{3/4}$
and $\delta\Lambda\propto V^{-1/4}$ there, rather than the Poisson value; the
geometry only fixes the amplitude. For the smeared action this holds only
within a smaller range, and the physically relevant limit of strong smearing
remains open. Reading the action fluctuation as a fluctuation of the
cosmological term, and assuming that the exponent and the temporal
correlation measured for the local action carry over to the strongly smeared
one, we confront stochastic $\Lambda(t)$ histories with Pantheon+SH0ES
supernovae, DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations and Planck distance
priors. With the measured correlation, none of $10^4$ histories fits as well
as $\Lambda$CDM ($\Delta\chi^2_{\rm min}=+74$ with all three probes, $+23$
without the CMB).
Within a wider family of dynamics, rare good histories appear only when
$\Lambda$ stays close to a slowly wandering constant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Causal set theory replaces the spacetime continuum by a locally finite
partially ordered set, whose elements are related by the causal order and
whose number counts spacetime volume~\cite{Bombelli1987,Surya2019}. A
heuristic argument by Sorkin~\cite{Sorkin1997,Sorkin2007b}, made precise in
the Everpresent $\Lambda$ model~\cite{Ahmed2004,Ahmed2013,Das2023,Das2024},
connects this discreteness with the
cosmological constant. In unimodular gravity $\Lambda$ is conjugate to the
spacetime volume $V$; if $V$ is only defined up to the Poisson fluctuation
$\delta N=\sqrt{N}$ of the number of elements, then $\Lambda$ fluctuates by
$\delta\Lambda\sim V^{-1/2}\sim H^2$. The resulting dark energy density tracks
the critical density at all epochs, which would explain why $\Omega_\Lambda$
is of order one today without fine tuning.

The exponent $-1/2$ is essential to the model. If the fluctuation of the
action grows as $V^p$ (the exponent $p$ of $\Delta S$ used throughout), then
$\delta\Lambda\propto V^{p-1}$ and, with $V\sim H^{-4}$, then $\delta\Lambda/H^2\propto H^{2-4p}$, and
only $p=1/2$ gives an \emph{everpresent} $\Omega_\Lambda$. In the
phenomenological realisation of Das, Nasiri and Yazdi (Model~1 of
\cite{Das2023}), this exponent is imposed by construction: each causal-set
element contributes an independent random variable to the action term
$\Lambda V$. The authors note that ``the true causal set mechanism that gives
rise to Everpresent $\Lambda$ is not fully understood''~\cite{Das2023}.

A natural candidate for a mechanism is the causal-set action itself. The
Benincasa--Dowker (BD) action~\cite{Benincasa2010,Dowker2013} is a
discretisation of the Einstein--Hilbert action, built from counts of
intervals, whose mean reproduces the Einstein--Hilbert action in the
continuum limit, up to boundary terms~\cite{Buck2015,Dowker2025}. Moradi, Yazdi and Zilh\~ao (MYZ)~\cite{Moradi2025} developed
a formalism for the variance of this action under Poisson sprinkling and
raised the question of whether its fluctuations could be the origin of
Everpresent $\Lambda$. They expressed the variance as a set of integrals over
pairs of intervals but did not evaluate them.

Fluctuations are a known issue for causal-set operators. The fluctuations of
the local causal-set d'Alembertian at a point grow with the density, which
motivated the smeared, non-local operators~\cite{Sorkin2007,Benincasa2010};
simulations indicate that the fluctuations of the smeared operator die away
at fixed non-locality scale~\cite{Benincasa2010,Belenchia2016c}, and the
stability of the continuum operators has been studied
in~\cite{Aslanbeigi2014}. Efficient codes compute the BD action of large
sprinkled causal sets~\cite{Cunningham2018}. For the action of a region, as
opposed to the operator at a point, Benincasa, Dowker and Schmitzer called the
behaviour of the fluctuations at large density ``a major open
question''~\cite{Benincasa2011}: preliminary two-dimensional simulations
showed them growing with the density~\cite{Schmitzer2010}, and the authors
suggested that a mesoscale might be needed to tame them. To our knowledge,
the variance and its scaling with the number of elements have not been
computed beyond these preliminary numerics.

In this paper we compute them and follow the consequences through to
cosmological data. Our main results are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{same-pair} term of the variance, the Poisson noise in the
number of nearly null intervals, grows asymptotically as $(\rhoV)^{2-2/d}$,
with a prefactor set by a boost-range integral $G$ of the region that we
compute in closed form or by quadrature. We derive the leading correction,
of relative order $(\rhoV)^{-1/2}\ln\rhoV$ in four dimensions, and verify both
against exact means up to $\rhoV=10^8$ (section~\ref{sec:boost} and
appendix~\ref{app:boost}).
\item In all the regions we simulate (causal diamonds, truncated past light
cones and past cones of flat FLRW spacetimes reaching the big bang), the
remaining correlation terms are a constant fraction of the same-pair term
within the measured range ($\rhoV\le25\,600$) for the local action
(section~\ref{sec:numerics}). The exponent of $\Delta S$ is therefore
consistent with $3/4$ in four dimensions, not with the Poisson value $1/2$.
For the smeared action this holds within a smaller range of $\epsilon\rhoV$,
with a weak residual trend for the strongest smearing.
\item If the action fluctuation is read as a fluctuation of the cosmological
term, $\delta\Lambda\propto V^{-1/4}$ (section~\ref{sec:lambda}). Only the
strongly smeared action has an acceptable amplitude, so the data test assumes
that the exponent and the temporal correlation that we measure for the local
action, the latter along nested past cones, carry over to it. With this
assumption we confront stochastic $\Lambda(t)$ histories with supernova, BAO
and CMB data. They are excluded over the measured range of the memory, as is Model~1 in our implementation; within a wider family of
dynamics, rare good histories appear only when $\Lambda$ stays close to a
slowly wandering constant (section~\ref{sec:data}).
\end{enumerate}
The mechanism behind the first result is well known in another guise: the
non-compactness of the Lorentz group makes the number of links, or of
intervals of any fixed small cardinality, grow without bound in a sprinkled
causal set~\cite{Bombelli2009}, and the mean abundances of small intervals in
flat regions are known exactly~\cite{Glaser2013}. The same non-compact
rapidity range biases the statistics of links~\cite{Boguna2026}. What is new
here is its role in the \emph{fluctuations} of the action: we show that it
controls their scaling, give the prefactor in closed form as a boost-range
integral $G$ valid for any region, including FLRW past cones, quantify the
amplitude in the regions relevant for cosmology, and follow the consequences
for a fluctuating cosmological constant. In two dimensions this gives the
quantitative answer to the question of~\cite{Benincasa2011}: the fluctuations
grow as $\sqrt{\rhoV\ln\rhoV}$, and smearing reduces their amplitude but not
their growth.

\section{The BD action and its variance}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Sprinklings and the action}

A Poisson sprinkling of density $\rho$ into a region $M$ of a $d$-dimensional
Lorentzian manifold produces a causal set whose number of elements $N$ is a
Poisson variable with mean $\rhoV$, $V={\rm vol}(M)$. For a related pair
$y\prec x$ let $n(y,x)$ be the number of elements strictly between them, and
let $N_k$ be the number of pairs with $n=k$. We use the BD action in the form
of a sum over related pairs,
\begin{equation}
S=a_N N+\sum_{y\prec x} f\big(n(y,x)\big)\,,
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
which is the form analysed by MYZ~\cite{Moradi2025}. For the \emph{local}
action, $f(n)=a_n$ for $n<n_d$ and $f=0$ otherwise, with
\begin{equation}
d=2:\quad (a_N;a_0,a_1,a_2)=(1;-2,4,-2),\qquad
d=4:\quad (a_N;a_0,\dots,a_3)=\frac{4}{\sqrt6}(1;-1,9,-16,8).
\label{eq:coeffs}
\end{equation}
In four dimensions these are the coefficients of~\cite{Benincasa2010}, normalised
so that the mean action tends to $\frac{1}{2\ell^2}\int R\,\rmd V$ (plus
boundary terms~\cite{Buck2015,Dowker2025,Machet2021}), with $\ell=\rho^{-1/4}$.
The action of MYZ, their equation (4.1) evaluated at $\phi=-2\ell^2/\alpha_d$,
is $-\sqrt6/2$ times ours in four dimensions; this multiplies the variance by
$3/2$ and leaves the remainder $r$ and all exponents below unchanged. In two dimensions they are those
of~\cite{Benincasa2011}, for which the mean action of a flat causal diamond
tends to $1$, its Euler characteristic, up to corrections that vanish as
$\rho\to\infty$~\cite{Benincasa2011}. The \emph{smeared} action of
Sorkin~\cite{Sorkin2007} and Dowker and Glaser~\cite{Dowker2013,Glaser2014} introduces a
non-locality parameter $\epsilon\in(0,1]$, with $\epsilon=(\ell/\ell_k)^d$
for a non-locality length $\ell_k$. It has the same form~\eref{eq:action}
with
\begin{equation}
a_N=\epsilon^{2/d}a_N^{(1)},\qquad
f_\epsilon(n)=\epsilon^{1+2/d}\sum_{k<n_d}a_k^{(1)}
{n\choose k}\epsilon^k(1-\epsilon)^{n-k},
\label{eq:smeared}
\end{equation}
where $a^{(1)}$ are the local coefficients~\eref{eq:coeffs}; the overall
factor $\epsilon^{2/d}$ comes from $\ell_\epsilon^2=\ell^2/\epsilon^{2/d}$, and
the additional $\epsilon$ in $f_\epsilon$ from the normalisation of the smeared
operator~\cite{Dowker2013}. In four dimensions, expanding the binomials gives
$f_\epsilon(n)=-\frac{4}{\sqrt6}\epsilon^{3/2}(1-\epsilon)^n\big[1-\frac{9\epsilon
n}{1-\epsilon}+\frac{8\epsilon^2n(n-1)}{(1-\epsilon)^2}-\frac{4\epsilon^3n(n-1)(n-2)}
{3(1-\epsilon)^3}\big]$, the weight of the smeared d'Alembertian
of~\cite{Benincasa2010}. For $\epsilon=1$ the local action is recovered. The
binomial weight is the probability that a pair with $n$ elements in between
keeps exactly $k$ of them after thinning each with probability $\epsilon$, so
the smeared action can be written with ``thinned'' abundances
$\tilde N_k=\sum_{y\prec x}{n\choose k}\epsilon^k(1-\epsilon)^{n-k}$, and the
MYZ formalism applies to them with minor modifications
(appendix~\ref{app:estimators}).

\subsection{Means and the Mecke formula}

All the mean values we need follow from the Mecke formula for Poisson
processes~\cite{LastPenrose2017}. For any function $h$,
\begin{equation}
\Big\langle\sum_{y\prec x}h\big(n(y,x)\big)\Big\rangle
=\rho^2\int_M\rmd V_x\int_{M\cap J^-(x)}\rmd V_y\;
\mathbb{E}\,h\big(\mathrm{Poisson}(\rho\,{\rm vol}\,I(y,x))\big),
\label{eq:mecke}
\end{equation}
where $I(y,x)$ is the causal interval. In a flat causal diamond the inner
integral depends on $x$ only through the volume of its past within the
diamond, and~\eref{eq:mecke} reduces to two nested one-dimensional
quadratures. We also need the covariance of $N$ with the
pair sum $F=\sum_{y\prec x}f(n)$. Differentiating~\eref{eq:mecke} with respect
to the intensity gives (appendix~\ref{app:mecke})
\begin{equation}
{\rm Cov}(N,F)=\rho\,\frac{\rmd\langle F\rangle}{\rmd\rho}\,,
\label{eq:covNF}
\end{equation}
which for the local action reduces to the identity
${\rm Cov}(N,N_k)=(k+2)L_k-(k+1)L_{k+1}$ of MYZ, with $L_k=\langle N_k\rangle$.

\subsection{The MYZ decomposition}

The variance of~\eref{eq:action} is
\begin{equation}
(\Delta S)^2=a_N^2\,\rhoV+2a_N\,{\rm Cov}(N,F)+{\rm Var}(F)\,,
\label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
and MYZ decompose ${\rm Var}(F)$ according to how the two pairs
$(y_1,x_1)$, $(y_2,x_2)$ in $F^2$ share their endpoints:
\begin{equation}
{\rm Var}(F)=\Jone+J^2+J^3+2J^4+J^6_{\rm c}\,.
\label{eq:myz}
\end{equation}
Here $\Jone=\langle\sum_{y\prec x}f(n)^2\rangle$ is the \emph{same-pair} term;
$J^2$ and $J^3$ collect distinct pairs sharing their past or future endpoint;
$J^4$ collects chains $y_1\prec x_1=y_2\prec x_2$ (MYZ's $J^5$, the chain
$y_2\prec x_2=y_1\prec x_1$, is $J^4$ with the two pairs relabelled, so the two
are equal and absorbed in the factor $2$); and $J^6_{\rm c}$ is the
connected part of the contribution of pairs with four distinct endpoints,
which is non-zero only when the two intervals overlap. MYZ write all terms as
integrals over configurations of points, with the joint distribution of the
two interval cardinalities given by the volume of the intersection of the two
intervals.

We evaluated the MYZ integrals by Monte Carlo with an importance sampler
adapted to nearly null intervals. In four dimensions the intersection volume
of two causal intervals has no closed form; we computed it by quadrature,
using Green's theorem for the intersection of discs at fixed time and fixed
coordinate along one axis. In two dimensions the prediction agrees with
direct sprinklings at five densities $\rhoV=25$--$400$ ($|z|\le1.56$, 4000
sprinklings each) and, for the smeared action with $\epsilon=0.5,0.25$, at ten
more points ($|z|\le1.72$). In four dimensions the agreement holds for
$\rhoV=25,50,100$ (\tref{tab:validation} in appendix~\ref{app:validation}), but the Monte Carlo error grows quickly with density
(23\% at $\rhoV=100$). This makes the full MYZ integrals impractical as a
scaling tool, and in the rest of the paper we follow a different strategy:
we compute the terms that dominate asymptotically \emph{exactly}, and we
measure the remaining terms on sprinklings.

\section{The same-pair term and the boost range}
\label{sec:boost}

Consider a nearly null pair, with small proper time $\tau$ between its
endpoints. Its interval has volume $c_d\tau^d$ ($c_4=\pi/24$), so its
cardinality is a Poisson variable of mean $\mu=\rho c_d\tau^d$ that stays of
order one however large the region is. What grows with the size of the
region is the number of such pairs. Writing
$y=x-\tau(\cosh\chi,\sinh\chi\,\hat{\boldsymbol n})$, the measure of the past
hyperboloid is $\tau^{d-1}\sinh^{d-2}\chi\,\rmd\tau\,\rmd\chi\,\rmd\Omega$,
and the pair fits inside the region as long as its rapidity satisfies
$\tau\rme^\chi/2\lesssim T(x,\hat{\boldsymbol n})$, where
\begin{equation}
T(x,\hat{\boldsymbol n})=\sup\{\lambda:\ x-\lambda(1,\hat{\boldsymbol n})\in M\}
\label{eq:ell}
\end{equation}
is the extent, in time, of the past null ray from $x$ in the direction
$\hat{\boldsymbol n}$ that remains inside the region. The rapidity integral
then gives a factor $(T/\tau)^{d-2}$, which is the volume of the available
range of boosts. Inserting this in~\eref{eq:mecke} and changing variables to
$\mu$ gives, to leading order in $\rhoV$ and for $d=4$ (appendix~\ref{app:boost}),
\begin{equation}
\Big\langle\sum_{y\prec x}h(n)\Big\rangle\simeq(\rhoV)^{3/2}\,
\frac{G/V^{3/2}}{8\sqrt{c_4}}\,\sum_{n\ge0}h(n)\,\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac12)}{n!}\,,
\qquad
G=\int_M\rmd V_x\int_{S^2}\rmd\Omega\;T(x,\hat{\boldsymbol n})^2\,,
\label{eq:asym4}
\end{equation}
where $\rmd V_x$ is the physical volume element (in FLRW, $a(\eta_x)^4\rmd^4x$).
In general dimension the exponent is $2-2/d$, with a weight
$\Gamma(n+2/d)/n!$. Two special cases are needed below.

\paragraph{Two dimensions.} For $d=2$ the boost volume is
$\ln(T/\tau)$ and the weight is $\Gamma(n+1)/n!=1$, so that
$\langle\sum h\rangle=\rhoV\ln\rhoV\sum_n h(n)+O(\rhoV)$. The same-pair term
therefore contributes $\sum_nf(n)^2$ ($=24$ for the local action) to the
coefficient of $\rhoV\ln\rhoV$ in the variance. In two dimensions the
connected term $J^6_{\rm c}$ grows with the same logarithm (section~\ref{sec:numerics}), so
the variance is Poissonian up to a logarithm,
$(\Delta S)^2\simeq b\,\rhoV\ln\rhoV$, with $b=(1+r_2)\sum_nf(n)^2$ and $r_2$
the two-dimensional analogue of the remainder defined in
section~\ref{sec:numerics}.

\paragraph{The cross term.} For $h(n)=f(n)$ the leading term
of~\eref{eq:asym4} vanishes. For the local action in $d=4$,
$\sum_k a_k\Gamma(k+\frac12)/(k!\sqrt\pi)\propto-1+\frac92-6+\frac52=0$. This is
the same cancellation that makes the mean action finite in flat space, and
by~\eref{eq:covNF} it implies that ${\rm Cov}(N,F)$ grows only like $\rhoV$.

\medskip
Therefore, if the correlation terms in~\eref{eq:myz} do not grow faster than
$\Jone$, the variance is dominated by
\begin{equation}
\Jone\propto(\rhoV)^{2-2/d}\sum_n f(n)^2\,\frac{\Gamma(n+2/d)}{n!}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta S\propto(\rhoV)^{1-1/d},
\label{eq:pexp}
\end{equation}
that is, $\Delta S\propto(\rhoV)^{3/4}$ in four dimensions. The region enters
only through $G$, the integrated squared length of the past null rays: the
boost range available to nearly null intervals. The exponent is a direct
consequence of Lorentz invariance together with a cut-off set by the size of
the region, the same non-compactness that makes the number of links unbounded
in a sprinkling~\cite{Bombelli2009}; it cannot be changed by a finite
rescaling of the density.

\paragraph{Why the correlation terms should follow $\Jone$.} We have no proof
that the other terms in~\eref{eq:myz} grow no faster than $\Jone$. The
underlying claim is the same for all of them: for a given nearly null pair,
the measure of other pairs whose weights are correlated with it is finite,
i.e.\ it does not itself grow with the boost range. For $J^6_{\rm c}$, which
only receives contributions from overlapping intervals, this is plausible:
two nearly null intervals overlap appreciably only if they lie along nearly
the same null direction with comparable rapidity, so $J^6_{\rm c}$ should
inherit the boost range once, like $\Jone$. For $J^2$, $J^3$ and $J^4$, which
are built from the row and column sums of the matrix of pair weights
(appendix~\ref{app:estimators}), e.g.\ $R_y=\sum_x f(y,x)$, a necessary
condition holds exactly: the mean of $R_y$ has no boost-enhanced part, since
its leading term is proportional to $\sum_nf(n)\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!$, which
vanishes by the cancellation just described. This removes the obvious way in
which these terms could acquire a second factor of the boost range, but it is
not sufficient, because $J^2$ involves the covariance of two pairs that share
$y$, which does not cancel when both lie along nearly the same null direction.
We have not estimated these finite measures analytically. In two dimensions
the claim is borne out, with an $O(1)$ coefficient that grows as $\epsilon$
decreases: the correlation terms grow with the same logarithm as $\Jone$ and
$r_2$ is finite (section~\ref{sec:numerics}). The numerical tests of the next
section probe it directly in four dimensions.

The approach to the asymptotic regime is slow. The exact $\Jone$ of the local
action in the four-dimensional diamond, computed from~\eref{eq:mecke} without
Monte Carlo, has a local exponent $\frac12\,\rmd\ln\Jone/\rmd\ln\rhoV$ that
decreases from $1.26$ at $\rhoV\approx35$ to $0.96$ at $\rhoV\approx570$,
$0.88$ at $\rhoV\approx2300$ and $0.77$ at $\rhoV\approx3\times10^5$.
Exponents measured at small densities, such as the $\Delta S\propto
(\rhoV)^{1.2}$ we find for $\rhoV\le100$, are therefore pre-asymptotic. The
slow approach is that of the leading correction to~\eref{eq:asym4}, which we
derive in appendix~\ref{app:boost}: the exact mean is the asymptotic one
times $1-(\rhoV)^{-1/2}(a_n\ln\rhoV+b_n)+\dots$, with
\begin{equation}
a_n=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{c_4}\,G/V^{3/2}}\,\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\frac12)}
\label{eq:corr}
\end{equation}
fixed by the measure of the hyperboloid and $b_n$ dependent on the region.

\section{Numerical tests}
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Method}

For each region we compute $\Jone$, $\langle F\rangle$ and ${\rm Cov}(N,F)$
exactly from~\eref{eq:mecke} and~\eref{eq:covNF}, measure the total variance
on independent Poisson sprinklings, and define the correlation
\emph{remainder}
\begin{equation}
r=\frac{{\rm Var}(F)-\Jone}{\Jone}
=\frac{J^2+J^3+2J^4+J^6_{\rm c}}{\Jone}\,,
\label{eq:rest}
\end{equation}
the relative size of all correlation terms. The asymptotic
exponent~\eref{eq:pexp} holds if $r$ tends to a constant. Our criterion is
that the slope of a weighted linear fit of $r$ against $\ln\rhoV$ be
compatible with zero at $4\sigma$. A slope compatible with zero is a weak
statement on its own, so we also quote the bound it implies on a residual
power law, $r\propto(\rhoV)^\delta$: to first order the slope equals
$\delta\,\bar r$. Such a law would change the asymptotic exponent to
$p=3/4+\delta/2$ once the correlations dominate. The $4\sigma$ threshold
serves as a pass/fail criterion that guards against false alarms among many
comparisons; the quantitative statement is the $2\sigma$ bound on $\delta$.
Each sprinkling is an exact, independent sample of the ensemble; unlike
Markov-chain sampling, no thermalisation is needed. The statistical error of a
variance estimated from $M$ sprinklings is ${\rm std}[(S-\bar S)^2]/\sqrt M$.

\paragraph{Methodological note.} All pass/fail thresholds ($4\sigma$, the
tolerance of every automated test of this work) were fixed before the
corresponding runs; for the causal diamond, after a first set of five
densities and before the larger ones. The points added after independent
reviews of earlier drafts ($\rhoV=12\,800$ and $25\,600$ in the diamond,
$6400$ in the FLRW cones, and the tests called additional below) also had
their criteria fixed in advance. The criteria that failed are reported where
they arise: the original and the extended criteria for the smeared action
(section~\ref{sec:smeared}), a bound on the memory of the action
(section~\ref{sec:lambda}) and the reproduction of a published $\chi^2$
(section~\ref{sec:data}).

\subsection{Causal diamond}

\Fref{fig:diamond} shows the four-dimensional diamond with the local action,
for $\rhoV=25$ to $6400$ (500 to 6000 sprinklings per density). The total
variance follows the exact $\Jone$ with a constant offset. The local
exponent of the total variance decreases with $\rhoV$ like that of $\Jone$,
from $1.24\pm0.04$ at $\rhoV\approx35$ to $0.72\pm0.05$ at $\rhoV\approx4500$;
between $12\,800$ and $25\,600$ (below) it is $0.71\pm0.07$. The last two
values lie below those of $\Jone$ ($0.85$ and $0.81$; $2.6\sigma$ and
$1.5\sigma$), which is the direction a slowly decreasing $r$ would produce.
Local exponents from neighbouring densities are noisy, and the fit of the
remainder, below, is the more robust statement; its bound on a decreasing $r$
covers this deviation. On these
sprinklings alone, $r=0.317\pm0.014$ with slope $+0.006\pm0.009$. As an
additional test (see the methodological note) we combined them with the independent sprinklings of the
smeared-action runs below (at $\epsilon=1$, $\rhoV=400$--$6400$) and with 500
new sprinklings at each of $\rhoV=12\,800$ and $25\,600$. The remainder at
these densities is $r=0.38\pm0.10$ and $0.19\pm0.08$, compatible with the
earlier mean ($0.6\sigma$ and $-1.7\sigma$), and over the whole range
$r=0.321\pm0.012$, i.e.\ $(\Delta S)^2\simeq1.32\,\Jone$, with slope
$+0.002\pm0.007$ ($0.3\sigma$; \fref{fig:diamond}(c)). The fit has
$\chi^2=16.0$ for 9 degrees of freedom ($p$-value $0.07$): the points scatter
about $30\%$ more than their formal errors, as is visible between
$\rhoV=800$ and $6400$ in \fref{fig:diamond}(c). Scaling the slope error by
$\sqrt{\chi^2/{\rm dof}}$, the bound on a residual power law is
$-0.05<\delta<0.06$ at $2\sigma$ ($-0.11<\delta<0.12$ at $4\sigma$), i.e.\
$p\le0.78$ ($0.81$); with the unscaled errors it would be
$-0.04<\delta<0.05$. Two different but unbiased estimators of ${\rm Var}\,F$
are combined here: for the first set, the variance of the full action minus
the exact $N$ contributions; for the others, the variance of $F$ itself.
Since the per-density errors, ${\rm std}(d^2)/\sqrt M$, could be
underestimated for a heavy-tailed $d^2$, we checked them a posteriori with a
bootstrap over sprinklings wherever the individual sprinklings were stored:
the two errors agree within $3\%$ in all 39 sets (\texttt{figures/goal6\_bootstrap.json}
in the repository), so the excess scatter is not an underestimate of the
individual errors. The bound is also stable against the fitted range:
restricting the fit to $\rhoV\ge400$ gives $\delta<0.04$ ($2\sigma$; $0.09$
with the scaled error, $\chi^2=12.6$ for 5 degrees of freedom). An exponent as low as the Poisson value would require
$r\to-1$, correlations that cancel $\Jone$; we see no trend in that direction.

In two dimensions the logarithmic law of section~\ref{sec:boost} holds. For
the diamond with $\rhoV=50$--$12\,800$, computed from the MYZ integrals (which
agree with sprinklings wherever both are available, up to $\rhoV=3200$), the
slope of $(\Delta S)^2/\rhoV$ against $\ln\rhoV$ is $24.43\pm0.24$ for the
local action. Fitting each MYZ term separately over the same range, $\Jone$
contributes $23.2$, still approaching its asymptotic coefficient
$\sum_nf(n)^2=24$, and the correlation terms, essentially $J^6_{\rm c}$,
contribute $1.76\pm0.24$. These coefficients are not additive
($23.2+1.76\ne24.43$) because the exact $\Jone$ and the measured terms are
fitted with different weights, which emphasise different densities while
$\Jone$ is still curving towards its limit. Adding the correlation
coefficient to the asymptotic $\sum_nf(n)^2$ gives the estimate
$b\approx25.8$, i.e.\ $r_2\approx0.07\pm0.01$, and not $\sum_nf(n)^2$
alone. For the
smeared action the correlations are larger, $r_2=0.42\pm0.04$ for
$\epsilon=0.5$ and $0.60\pm0.06$ for $\epsilon=0.25$, a growth with
decreasing $\epsilon$ similar to that of $r$ in four dimensions
(section~\ref{sec:smeared}). Binning the
pairs that contribute to $\sum f^2$ by their rapidity
$\eta=\frac12\ln(\Delta u/\Delta v)$ gives a plateau for $|\eta|\lesssim3$,
cut off at $|\eta|\approx\frac12\ln\rhoV$: every unit of rapidity contributes
the same, which identifies the logarithm with the boost range rather than with
the boundary.

\subsection{Past light cones and FLRW}

Everpresent $\Lambda$ involves the causal past of an observer, not a diamond.
We therefore repeated the analysis for the truncated past light cone
$J^-(q)\cap\{t\ge0\}$ in Minkowski spacetime and for the past light cone of an
observer in a spatially flat FLRW spacetime, $\rmd s^2=a(\eta)^2(-\rmd\eta^2
+\rmd\boldsymbol x^2)$, reaching the big bang at $\eta=0$. We take
$a=\eta^\alpha$ with $\alpha=1$ (radiation) and $\alpha=2$ (matter). In FLRW
the interval volumes are computed exactly: at fixed $\eta$ the section of a
causal interval is a lens (the intersection of two balls), and the integral
over $\eta$ is done exactly by Gauss--Legendre quadrature in each polynomial
piece. The boost-range integral of~\eref{eq:asym4} becomes
\begin{equation}
G=\int\rmd V_x\int\rmd\Omega\int_0^{\eta_x}
\frac{2\lambda\,a(\eta_x-\lambda)^4}{\sqrt{w}}\,\rmd\lambda,\qquad
w=\frac{6}{\lambda^3}\int_0^\lambda a(\eta_y+s)^4\,s(\lambda-s)\,\rmd s,
\label{eq:Gflrw}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the conformal extent of a nearly null interval along the
null ray, $\eta_y=\eta_x-\lambda$ the conformal time of its past endpoint, and
$w$ is the average of $a^4$ over it, weighted by the profile
$s(\lambda-s)$ of a thin four-dimensional diamond. For $a=1$ it reduces to the
Minkowski cone.

\subsection{Smeared action}
\label{sec:smeared}

The smeared action is designed to suppress fluctuations, and it is the
version that can give a phenomenologically relevant amplitude
(section~\ref{sec:lambda}). For fixed $\epsilon$ the argument of
section~\ref{sec:boost} applies unchanged, with
$\Jone_\epsilon\propto(\rhoV)^{3/2}\sum_nf_\epsilon(n)^2\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!$,
and $\Jone_\epsilon$ is again computed exactly from~\eref{eq:mecke}. Whether
the correlation terms remain proportional to it is less clear, because the
smeared action receives contributions from intervals with up to
$\sim1/\epsilon$ elements, so the asymptotic regime requires
$\epsilon\rhoV\gg1$.

We sprinkled the four-dimensional diamond at $\rhoV=400$--$6400$ (2000 to 500
sprinklings per density) and analysed the same sprinklings with
$\epsilon=1,0.5,0.2,0.1$; the decomposition described below uses a second,
independent set of sprinklings of the same sizes. The exact means and covariances~\eref{eq:covNF}
agree with the simulation in all 40 comparisons ($|z|\le2.35$), and
$\epsilon=1$ reproduces the local result, $r=0.345\pm0.025$.

Our pre-registered criterion for the smeared action was formulated for the
remainder \emph{after} subtracting the part of $F$ linear in $N$ (below),
$r_\perp=r-\rhoV g^2/\Jone$. It was met for $\epsilon=0.5$ (slope
$0.9\sigma$) but failed for $\epsilon=0.2$ and $0.1$, where $r_\perp$ grows
with $\ln\rhoV$ at $4.1\sigma$ and $6.7\sigma$. The following analysis,
designed after this failure, explains it.

For $\epsilon\le0.2$ two contributions of different origin compete at these
densities. The part of $F$ that is linear in $N$, of variance
$\rhoV\,g^2$ with $g=\rmd\langle F\rangle/\rmd(\rhoV)$, is Poissonian, so it
decays relative to $\Jone$ as $(\rhoV)^{-1/2}$; for $\epsilon=0.1$ it falls
from $2.75\,\Jone$ at $\rhoV=400$ to $0.55\,\Jone$ at $6400$. To separate the
contributions we measured every MYZ term directly on each sprinkling, using
estimators built from the matrix $f(y,x)$ of pair weights and its row and
column sums (appendix~\ref{app:estimators}). The generic MYZ integrator is not usable
in this regime: at $\rhoV=6400$ its error on $J^6_{\rm c}$ was about ten times
the signal even with $4\times10^5$ samples.

\Fref{fig:smeared} shows the result. As a check, the estimated $\Jone$ agrees
with the exact value in all fifteen cases ($|z|\le0.86$), and at $\rhoV=400$,
$\epsilon=0.1$, the estimated $J^4=17.86\pm0.06$ agrees with the independent
MYZ Monte Carlo value $19.4\pm2.1$. For $\epsilon=0.5$
all terms are flat, with $r\simeq1.3$. For $\epsilon=0.2$ and $0.1$ the terms
with a shared endpoint, which carry the Poissonian part, decrease relative to
$\Jone$, while $J^6_{\rm c}$ increases (from $-3.7\,\Jone$ to
$-0.1\,\Jone$ for $\epsilon=0.1$). The two trends compensate: the total remainder
stays at $r\approx3.0$ ($\epsilon=0.2$) and $r\approx4.5$ ($\epsilon=0.1$), with
slopes against $\ln\rhoV$ of $+0.17\pm0.08$ and $-0.29\pm0.11$, both below
$3\sigma$. The growth of $r_\perp$ that made the pre-registered criterion
fail is therefore not a growing correlation: it is the approach of $r_\perp$
to $r$ as the subtracted Poissonian term decays.

We tested this explanation with a new criterion: the slope of the unprojected
$r$, combining both sets of sprinklings with 500 new ones at each of
$\rhoV=12\,800$ and $25\,600$ (which extend $\epsilon\rhoV$ to $2560$),
must be compatible with zero at $4\sigma$, and the new points with the
earlier mean. At $\rhoV=25\,600$ we
find $r=1.41\pm0.16$, $3.33\pm0.25$ and $3.99\pm0.32$ for $\epsilon=0.5$,
$0.2$ and $0.1$, all compatible with the earlier means ($0.5\sigma$,
$1.7\sigma$, $-1.9\sigma$). The slopes over $\rhoV=400$--$25\,600$ are
$+0.02\pm0.02$ ($1.0\sigma$), $+0.12\pm0.04$ ($2.9\sigma$) and
$-0.24\pm0.06$ ($-4.1\sigma$, $\chi^2=9.6$ for 5 degrees of freedom). The
criterion is therefore met for $\epsilon=0.5$ and $0.2$ but \emph{not} for
$\epsilon=0.1$, where $r$ decreases. Much of this trend comes from the point
at $\rhoV=400$, where $\epsilon\rhoV=40$ is presumably pre-asymptotic: for
$\rhoV\ge800$ the slopes are $+0.01\pm0.03$, $+0.17\pm0.06$ ($3.0\sigma$) and
$-0.12\pm0.07$ ($-1.6\sigma$). The implied bounds on a residual power law at
$2\sigma$ are $-0.02<\delta<0.05$, $0.01<\delta<0.07$ and
$-0.08<\delta<-0.03$. At $\rhoV=25\,600$ the bootstrap errors agree with
the formula within $3\%$.

These trends have opposite signs and different consequences. For
$\epsilon=0.2$ the remainder may grow slightly; a power law with
$\delta\le0.07$ would raise the asymptotic exponent to at most $p\approx0.79$,
away from the Poisson value. For $\epsilon=0.1$ it decreases: the
correlations grow more slowly than $\Jone$, which leaves $p=3/4$ if the
decrease saturates or $r$ decays to zero, but which, extrapolated linearly
in $\ln\rhoV$, would eventually let the (negative) correlations cancel part
of $\Jone$. Within the accessible range, $\epsilon\rhoV\le2560$, the smeared
action is therefore compatible with $\Delta S\propto(\rhoV)^{3/4}$, but it
has not reached a clean asymptotic regime: the individual terms are still
drifting and the total shows weak trends of both signs. We cannot exclude a
different behaviour for $\epsilon\to0$ at fixed $\epsilon\rhoV$; we return to
this point in section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Consequences for a fluctuating $\Lambda$}
\label{sec:lambda}

\subsection{Amplitude}

To connect the action with $\Lambda$ we follow the logic of Everpresent
$\Lambda$; the relation below is a working hypothesis, not a consequence of a
causal-set dynamics, and the comparison with Model~1 that follows only checks
its normalisation. The BD action approximates the Einstein--Hilbert action,
$S_{\rm EH}/\hbar=\kappa S$ with $\kappa=\ell^2/(8\pi\ell_p^2)$ in our
normalisation, and the cosmological term is $\Lambda V/(8\pi\ell_p^2)$
\cite{Das2023}. Reading the action fluctuation in the past of the observer as a
fluctuation of the cosmological term gives
\begin{equation}
\delta\Lambda\,\ell^2=\frac{\Delta S}{\rhoV}\,,
\label{eq:dlambda}
\end{equation}
independently of $\ell_p$. Here $\delta\Lambda$ is a standard deviation: the
action fluctuation has zero mean, and, as in Model~1, it sets the rms of a
zero-mean stochastic $\Lambda$. In Model~1 the fluctuating part of the
action is Poissonian, $\Delta S_{\rm EH}/\hbar=\sqrt N/2$, and the discreteness
length is $\ell^2=\ell_p^2/(2\alpha)$ (equations 3.9 and 3.11--3.13
of~\cite{Das2023}). Then $\kappa=1/(16\pi\alpha)$,
$\Delta S=8\pi\alpha\sqrt N$, and~\eref{eq:dlambda} gives
$\delta\Lambda\,\ell^2=8\pi\alpha/\sqrt N$, i.e.\ its
$\delta\Lambda=8\pi\alpha/\sqrt V$, as it should, since Model~1 defines its
fluctuating term through the same relation.

A further question concerns boundaries. The mean action of a region with a
boundary contains boundary and corner
terms~\cite{Buck2015,Dowker2025,Machet2021}, which do not enter here because
we use only the fluctuation. The fluctuation itself, however, depends on the
boundary through $G$: the number of nearly null pairs is set by where the
past null rays leave the region. One may therefore ask whether it should be
read as a fluctuation of the bulk cosmological term. The pairs that produce
it are not concentrated near the boundary: they start at points $x$
throughout the region, the integrand $T(x,\hat{\boldsymbol n})^2$ of $G$ is
largest deep inside it, and in the two-dimensional diamond every unit of
rapidity contributes equally (section~\ref{sec:numerics}). In this sense the
fluctuation is a bulk effect whose range is set by the region, rather than a
boundary term; but its reading as $\delta\Lambda$ remains an identification.
With the results of section~\ref{sec:numerics},
\begin{equation}
\delta\Lambda\,\ell^2=A\,(\rhoV)^{-1/4},\qquad
A^2=(1+r)\,\frac{G/V^{3/2}}{8\sqrt{c_4}}\sum_nf(n)^2\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac12)}{n!}\,.
\label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}
For the causal past, back to the big bang, of a comoving observer today in a
Planck 2018 $\Lambda$CDM background~\cite{Planck2018}, computed by quadrature
in conformal time, $V\approx6.4\times10^{242}\,\ell_p^4$. With
the local action and $\ell=\ell_p$ this gives
$\delta\Lambda/\Lambda_{\rm obs}\sim10^{62}$; matching the observed value would
require $\ell\sim10^{27}\,$m, larger than the Hubble radius. The local action is
therefore excluded as a source.

For the smeared action we find numerically that
$\sum_nf_\epsilon(n)^2\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!\to4.112\,\epsilon^{5/2}$ as
$\epsilon\to0$, so $A\propto\epsilon^{5/4}=(\ell/\ell_k)^5$. The observed
$\Lambda$ is obtained for $\ell_k\approx2.6\times10^{-23}\,$m with
$\ell=\ell_p$ ($\epsilon\sim10^{-49}$), or $\ell_k\approx1.25\times10^{-22}\,$m
with $\ell=\ell_{\rm cs}=7.1\,\ell_p$, the discreteness length that corresponds
to $\alpha=0.01$ in Model~1 ($\ell^2=\ell_p^2/2\alpha$). These values use $r=0.33$; since
$\ell_k\propto(1+r)^{1/10}$, the larger $r\approx3$--$4.5$ found above for small
$\epsilon$ raises them by at most $15\%$. This is consistent with the bound
$\ell_k\lesssim10^{-19}\,$m on the non-locality scale of relativistic
non-local field theories derived from $8\,$TeV LHC data~\cite{Biswas2015},
as quoted in~\cite{Belenchia2016}, and inside the range, from $2\times10^{-22}$ to
$10^{-29}\,$m, that proposed optomechanical experiments could
probe~\cite{Belenchia2016}. It
requires, however, a large hierarchy $\ell_k/\ell_p\sim10^{12}$, so the
coincidence problem that Everpresent $\Lambda$ was meant to solve reappears as
a tuning of $\ell_k$. Equation~\eref{eq:amplitude} also changes the
\emph{time} dependence: since $\delta\Lambda\propto V^{-1/4}\propto H$ in
the matter era, the envelope $\delta\Lambda/3H^2$ behaves as a fluid with
$w\approx-1/2$, negligible at recombination ($\sim4\times10^{-5}$ at $z=1100$
for the amplitude that matches the observed $\Lambda$ today) and growing until
today, whereas for Model~1 $w\approx0$.

\subsection{Temporal correlation along the worldline}

$\Delta S$ is a standard deviation over sprinklings, whereas $\Lambda(t)$
fluctuates in time along a single history. The bridge is the correlation of
the action between the past cones of two instants of the same worldline: it
turns the ensemble variance at each volume into the variance of a stochastic
process along the worldline. These cones are nested,
and since the region is causally convex every pair of the smaller cone is a
pair of the larger one with the same cardinality. A single sprinkling of the
largest cone therefore gives the action of all the nested cones. We measured it
for the local action in four dimensions, in the Minkowski cone and in the
matter-dominated FLRW cone (4000 sprinklings each, $\rhoV=200$, $500$, $1250$
and $3200$). The action is close to a random walk, with a small positive
memory: the correlation between the action and its increment is
$\gamma=0.069\pm0.015$ (weighted mean of the six pairs of adjacent cones;
no individual pair reaches $4\sigma$). We parametrise the correlation between
the actions $S_i$, $S_j$ of two nested cones, $V_i<V_j$, as
\begin{equation}
{\rm corr}(S_i,S_j)=\left(\frac{{\rm Var}\,S_i}{{\rm Var}\,S_j}\right)^{\beta/2},
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
where $\beta=1$ is a random walk with independent increments. The six pairs of
cones give $\beta$ between $0.88$ and $0.97$, with bootstrap errors of
$0.03$--$0.09$; combining them with their bootstrap covariance gives
$\beta=0.93\pm0.02$ (Minkowski) and $0.92\pm0.02$ (FLRW). The distribution of
the action over sprinklings is close to Gaussian at these densities (excess
kurtosis $0.7$ at $\rhoV=200$, compatible with zero at $3200$; below $0.2$ in
absolute value for the increments), which supports the Gaussian process
used below. A pre-registered requirement that
$|\gamma|<0.2$ for every pair of cones failed for one non-adjacent pair in
each geometry ($\gamma=0.29\pm0.12$ and $0.23\pm0.09$, within $1\sigma$ of
the bound); it does not affect the value of $\beta$.

\section{Confronting the data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Stochastic model}

Section~\ref{sec:lambda} excludes the local action by its amplitude, so the
test of this section concerns the strongly smeared action
($\epsilon\sim10^{-49}$). For it we assume that the exponent $q=1/4$ and the
memory $\beta$ measured for the local action carry over; neither has been
measured in that regime (section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

We model the cosmological constant along the observer's worldline as
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(t)=\Lambda_0\,V(t)^{-q}\,s(t),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $V(t)$ is the volume of the causal past and $s$ is an
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process~\cite{Uhlenbeck1930} in $\ln V$ with unit
variance and ${\rm corr}(s_1,s_2)=(V_1/V_2)^c$, i.e.\ with relaxation rate
$c$ per unit of $\ln V$. Model~1 of
\cite{Das2023} corresponds to $q=c=1/2$ and $\Lambda_0=8\pi\alpha$. The BD
action, with~\eref{eq:dlambda}, corresponds to $q=1/4$ and $c=3\beta/4$, and we
take $\beta=0.9$ from the measurement above. The background is integrated with
feedback, $(H/H_{100})^2=\omega_r a^{-4}+\omega_m a^{-3}+\Lambda/3$, with
$\Lambda$ in units of $H_{100}^2$ and $V$ accumulated along the way. We set
$\Lambda=0$ before $z=10^4$, start the process from zero and count
realisations with $H^2<0$ as collapsed. This is the convention that best
reproduces the completion fractions published for Model~1: 10\,000, 3258 and
575 out of 10\,000 for $\alpha=0.005$, $0.01$ and $0.012$, against 9990, 3228
and 535 in~\cite{Das2023}. The last two agree within binomial errors
($0.6\sigma$ and $1.7\sigma$); for $\alpha=0.005$ we find no collapse where
\cite{Das2023} report ten, a difference in rare early collapses that we have
not traced (it may lie in their random-number or start-up conventions). These
fractions are regenerated by the test suite.

We follow the per-seed method of \cite{Das2024}. Each seed fixes the random
numbers of the process, and for each seed we minimise $\chi^2$ over
$\omega_m$, the amplitude ($\alpha$ or $\Lambda_0$) and the supernova magnitude
$M_B$. The minimisation over $\omega_m$ and the amplitude uses a $10\times10$
logarithmic grid ($\omega_m=0.02$--$0.8$) with two local refinements, a final
resolution of about $5\%$ in $\omega_m$; $M_B$ is minimised analytically. We
then refine the 50 best seeds of each run with a continuous (Nelder--Mead)
minimisation. Because the CMB fixes $\omega_m$ to better than $1\%$, the
refinement lowers the minima substantially when the CMB is included (by up to
$\sim100$ for the best seed), and all values quoted below are refined. In
every run the 50th seed on the grid lies above the threshold by more than the
largest gain observed among the 50, so that, unless a seed gains more than
any of those, no seed outside them can become good. A seed is \emph{good} if
$\chi^2<\chi^2_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}+14.7$.
The margin is that implied by \cite{Das2024}, who count seeds with
$\chi^2<1500$ against their $\Lambda$CDM value of $1485.3$ for supernovae
alone. Our own supernova-only $\Lambda$CDM fit gives $1452.0$, with best-fit
parameters that agree with~\cite{Brout2022}; we could not reconstruct the
exact configuration behind $1485.3$, so we always apply the margin relative
to our own $\Lambda$CDM fit to the same data. The data are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pantheon+SH0ES supernovae~\cite{Scolnic2022,Brout2022,Riess2022}, with
the standard cut ($z_{\rm HD}>0.01$ or Cepheid host: 1657 of the 1701 light
curves, 77 of them calibrators) and the full statistical and systematic
covariance;
\item DESI DR2 BAO~\cite{DESI2025}: 13 measurements of $D_V/r_d$, $D_M/r_d$
and $D_H/r_d$ at 7 redshifts, with their covariance;
\item Planck 2018 distance priors~\cite{Chen2019} on $R$ and $l_A$, with
$\omega_b=0.02236$, the sound horizon integrated over each history, and
$z_*$, $z_{\rm drag}$ from the fits of \cite{HuSugiyama1996,EisensteinHu1998}
anchored to their Planck values.
\end{itemize}
The pipeline reproduces the Planck 2018 sound horizons to $0.08\%$ and $R$,
$l_A$ within $1\sigma$, and the DESI value of $\Omega_m$ from BAO alone
($0.2972$ against $0.2975\pm0.0086$). The treatment of radiation is
simplified (no massive neutrinos, fixed $\omega_r$), which induces a
systematic of order $0.1$--$0.2\%$ in $l_A$. A variant in which $l_A$ and
$r_d$ are rescaled so that the Planck best fit reproduces their published
values exactly gives the same conclusions. Distance priors assume standard
early physics. This holds for the BD model, where $\Lambda/3H^2\sim4\times10^{-5}$
at $z=1100$, but not for Model~1 ($\sim0.5$), which we therefore fit to
supernovae and BAO only. Its BAO term still uses the sound horizon
integrated over each history, which takes the fitting formula for
$z_{\rm drag}$~\cite{EisensteinHu1998} outside its range of validity, so
the BAO exclusion of Model~1 is less robust than that of the BD model, whose
$\Lambda$ is negligible before recombination. (With the CMB included anyway,
its minimum is $+359$.)

\subsection{Results}

With supernovae alone both models produce rare histories that fit as well as
$\Lambda$CDM: $0.25\%$ of good seeds for Model~1, consistent with the $\approx
0.3\%$ of \cite{Das2024}, and $0.48\%$ for the BD action. The good BD seeds
fix $\ell_k\approx3\times10^{-23}\,$m, consistent with the value inferred from
the amplitude alone. Both, however, prefer $\Omega_m\approx0.5$.

equation of state ($w_0w_a$, at $2.8$--$4.2\sigma$ depending on the supernova
sample~\cite{DESI2025}): that concerns a smooth drift of the mean, whereas the
models tested here are stochastic processes whose typical histories wander
far more than the data allow.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have evaluated the fluctuations of the BD action that MYZ left as an open
question. The central result is structural: in $d$ dimensions the variance is
dominated by the Poisson noise in the number of nearly null intervals, whose
number is controlled by the volume of the boost group that fits inside the
region, and this gives $\Delta S\propto(\rhoV)^{1-1/d}$. Only for $d=2$ is the
exponent $1/2$, and then with a logarithm. In four dimensions, for the local
action, the exponent is $3/4$ within the simulated range in every region we
tested, including the past cone of an FLRW observer reaching the big bang;
the smeared action is compatible with it over a smaller range, with weak
residual trends. If this persists asymptotically, a
cosmological constant sourced by these fluctuations would scale as
$\delta\Lambda\propto V^{-1/4}$, not as the $V^{-1/2}$ of Everpresent
$\Lambda$, and a stochastic history built on this scaling and on the
measured memory does not fit current data.

This does not refute Everpresent $\Lambda$ as such, whose original argument
relies on the Poisson fluctuation of $N$ and not on the action. It shows that
the action, the most natural dynamical quantity of the theory, does not
provide the required mechanism. The obstruction is Lorentz invariance itself:
any observable that counts intervals of fixed small volume inherits the boost
range of the region. The same non-compactness biases the statistics of links
in sprinkled causal sets~\cite{Boguna2026}. A mechanism that yields $V^{-1/2}$
would have to suppress the contribution of highly boosted intervals, for
instance through a boundary term or through a choice of frame that breaks
local Lorentz invariance.

Our conclusions have the following limitations.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Asymptotics.} The exponent $3/4$ rests on one exact ingredient
($\Jone\propto(\rhoV)^{3/2}$, verified up to $\rhoV=10^8$) and one empirical
one: that the correlations remain a constant fraction of $\Jone$. We verified
the latter up to $\rhoV=25\,600$ in the diamond and $6400$ in the past cones,
with a residual power law bounded by $-0.05<\delta<0.06$ ($2\sigma$, errors
scaled for the excess scatter). For the
smeared action, up to $\epsilon\rhoV=2560$, the pre-registered test failed
for $\epsilon=0.1$, where the remainder decreases ($-4.1\sigma$; $-1.6\sigma$
without the lowest density), and for $\epsilon=0.2$ it grows slightly
($2.9\sigma$); the individual MYZ terms still drift. The physically relevant
regime, $\rhoV\sim10^{242}$ with $\epsilon\sim10^{-49}$, is an extrapolation
over some 238 decades, which the heuristic argument of
section~\ref{sec:boost} supports but does not prove; for the strongly
non-local action that limit is, at present, a conjecture.
\item \emph{Identification.} Equation~\eref{eq:dlambda} is an assumption,
motivated by the cosmological term of the Einstein--Hilbert action and
consistent with Model~1, but not derived from a dynamics for causal sets.
\item \emph{Dynamics.} The data analysis uses a homogeneous $\Lambda(t)$ and
the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck family~\eref{eq:model}. Spatial fluctuations of
$\Lambda$ are further constrained by the isotropy of the
CMB~\cite{Barrow2007}; non-Gaussian processes or a different coupling
between the action and $\Lambda$ were not explored. The temporal memory was
measured for the local action only.
\item \emph{Statistics.} The per-seed fits use a grid search followed by a
continuous refinement of the 50 best seeds, rather than the MCMC
of~\cite{Das2024}. The refinement lowered the minima by up to $\sim100$ when
the CMB is included and produced good seeds where the grid had none, outside
the measured BD dynamics; a still finer treatment could lower the minima
further, although the BD minimum ($+52$ to $+74$) is far above the threshold.
Our
supernova-only $\Lambda$CDM $\chi^2$ differs from that of \cite{Das2024}
(section~\ref{sec:data}); all our margins are relative to our own fits.
\end{enumerate}
Earlier cosmological tests of Everpresent $\Lambda$ are those of
\cite{Ahmed2004,Zwane2017,Das2024}; to our knowledge this is the first
confrontation of Everpresent-type dynamics with the DESI DR2 BAO data.

\section{Covariance with the number of elements}
\label{app:mecke}

Let $\eta$ be a Poisson process of intensity $\rho$ on $M$ and $F(\eta)$ a
functional with finite second moment. The Mecke
equation~\cite[theorem~4.1]{LastPenrose2017} gives
$\mathbb{E}[N F]=\rho\int_M\mathbb{E}[F(\eta+\delta_x)]\,\rmd x$, while
differentiating with respect to the intensity gives
$\rho\,\rmd\mathbb{E}[F]/\rmd\rho=\rho\int_M\mathbb{E}[F(\eta+\delta_x)-
F(\eta)]\,\rmd x$. Subtracting $\mathbb{E}[N]\,\mathbb{E}[F]=\rhoV\,
\mathbb{E}[F]$ from the first expression yields~\eref{eq:covNF}, a standard
identity for Poisson functionals of Margulis--Russo type. For the local
action, $F=\sum_ka_kN_k$ and $\langle N_k\rangle$ depends on $\rho$ only through
$\rhoV$, which leads to ${\rm Cov}(N,N_k)=(k+2)L_k-(k+1)L_{k+1}$. We evaluate
$\rmd\langle F\rangle/\rmd\rho$ with a central difference and checked it
against this identity (relative error of ${\rm Cov}(N,F)$ below $10^{-7}$ for
the local action at $\rhoV=25$--$6400$) and against sprinklings of the
smeared action.

\section{Estimators of the MYZ terms}
\label{app:estimators}

\paragraph{Smeared action.} With thinned abundances the MYZ formalism carries
over with three changes: the means become
$\langle\tilde N_k\rangle=L_k(\epsilon\rhoV)/\epsilon^2$; $\Jone$ is no longer
diagonal in $k$, because the two factors of a single pair are independent
thinnings of the same interval; and in the joint distribution of two
overlapping intervals the overlap is split into points kept by both
(probability $\epsilon^2$) and points kept by only one ($\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$
each), since thinning a Poisson process produces independent Poisson
processes.

\paragraph{Per-sprinkling estimators.} Let $f(y,x)=f(n(y,x))$ for $y\prec x$
and $0$ otherwise, with row sums $R_y=\sum_xf(y,x)$ and column sums
$C_x=\sum_yf(y,x)$. Sorting the terms of $F^2$ by shared endpoints gives,
sprinkling by sprinkling,
\begin{equation}
\sum f^2\to\Jone,\quad
\sum_yR_y^2-\sum f^2\to J^2,\quad
\sum_xC_x^2-\sum f^2\to J^3,\quad
\sum_zC_zR_z\to J^4,
\end{equation}
and $J^6_{\rm c}$ is obtained by subtracting all of them from $(F-\langle
F\rangle)^2$. The expectation of each statistic is the corresponding MYZ term,
so the estimators are unbiased up to $O(1/M)$ from the sample mean. We checked
the decomposition against an explicit enumeration of all $43^2$ ordered pairs
of the 43 related pairs of a sprinkling with 29 elements ($\rhoV=40$,
$\epsilon=0.3$); the two agree to a relative precision of $10^{-9}$.

\section{The boost-range integral and its leading correction}
\label{app:boost}

\paragraph{Minkowski.} Fix $x$ and a unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol n}$, and
write the past point of a pair as
$y=x-\tau(\cosh\chi,\sinh\chi\,\hat{\boldsymbol n})$. In four dimensions the
measure is $\rmd V_y=\tau^3\sinh^2\chi\,\rmd\tau\,\rmd\chi\,\rmd\Omega$ and the
interval has volume $c_4\tau^4$. For $\chi\gg1$ the point $y$ lies at a
distance $\lambda=\tau\rme^\chi/2$ in time along the past null ray from $x$ in
the direction $\hat{\boldsymbol n}$, displaced from it by $O(\tau\rme^{-\chi})$,
so it lies in $M$ if $\lambda<T(x,\hat{\boldsymbol n})$ up to corrections
of relative order $\tau^2/T^2$. Then
\begin{equation}
\int_0^{\chi_{\max}}\sinh^2\chi\,\rmd\chi=\frac{\rme^{2\chi_{\max}}}{8}
-\frac{\chi_{\max}}{2}+O(1)
=\frac{T^2}{2\tau^2}-\frac12\ln\frac{2T}{\tau}+O(1),
\end{equation}
with $\rme^{\chi_{\max}}=2T/\tau$. The pairs with $\rme^\chi=O(1)$
contribute only to the $O(1)$ term. With $\mu=\rho c_4\tau^4$,
$\int_0^\infty\tau\,P_n(\mu)\,\rmd\tau=\frac14(\rho c_4)^{-1/2}\,
\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!$ and $\int_0^\infty\tau^3P_n(\mu)\,\rmd\tau=
\frac14(\rho c_4)^{-1}$, where $P_n(\mu)=\mu^n\rme^{-\mu}/n!$. Inserting the
leading term in~\eref{eq:mecke} gives~\eref{eq:asym4}:
$\langle N_n\rangle\simeq\rho^2\int\rmd V_x\int\rmd\Omega\,
\frac{\ell^2}{2}\,\frac14(\rho c_4)^{-1/2}\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!
=\rho^{3/2}\,G\,\Gamma(n+\frac12)/(8\sqrt{c_4}\,n!)$. The logarithm gives the
leading correction. Since $\ln(1/\tau)=\frac14\ln\rho+O(1)$ for the values of
$\tau$ that contribute, its ratio to the leading term is
\begin{equation}
-\frac{4\pi V\cdot\frac14(\rho c_4)^{-1}\cdot\frac14\ln\rho}
{G\cdot\frac14(\rho c_4)^{-1/2}\,\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!}
=-(\rhoV)^{-1/2}\,\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{c_4}\,G/V^{3/2}}\,\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\frac12)}\,
\ln\rhoV+O\big((\rhoV)^{-1/2}\big),
\end{equation}
which is~\eref{eq:corr}. The terms of order $(\rhoV)^{-1/2}$ without the
logarithm depend on $\int\rmd V\rmd\Omega\ln T$ and on the shape of the
boundary, and are absorbed in $b_n$. In two dimensions $\int\rmd\chi=
\chi_{\max}$ and the same steps give the logarithm of section~\ref{sec:boost}.

\paragraph{FLRW.} For $\rmd s^2=a(\eta)^2(-\rmd\eta^2+\rmd\boldsymbol x^2)$
we use conformal coordinates, in which the causal structure is that of
Minkowski spacetime. With $\lambda=\tau_c\rme^\chi/2$ the conformal extent of
the pair along the null ray and $\tau_c$ its conformal proper time, the
conformal measure becomes, for $\chi\gg1$,
$\tau_c^3\sinh^2\chi\,\rmd\tau_c\,\rmd\chi\,\rmd\Omega\simeq
\tau_c\lambda\,\rmd\tau_c\,\rmd\lambda\,\rmd\Omega$, and the physical measure
carries the factor $a(\eta_y)^4\simeq a(\eta_x-\lambda)^4$. The physical
volume of the thin interval is $c_4\tau_c^4\,w$, where $w$ is the average of
$a^4$ over it. A thin four-dimensional diamond of null extent $\lambda$ has
its volume distributed along the null direction with the profile
$s(\lambda-s)$, which gives the $w$ of~\eref{eq:Gflrw}. The integral over
$\tau_c$ is the Minkowski one with $c_4$ replaced by $c_4w$, i.e.\
$\frac14(\rho c_4w)^{-1/2}\Gamma(n+\frac12)/n!$, so that
\begin{equation}
\langle N_n\rangle\simeq\frac{\rho^{3/2}}{8\sqrt{c_4}}\,
\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac12)}{n!}\int\rmd V_x\int\rmd\Omega\int_0^{T}
\frac{2\lambda\,a(\eta_x-\lambda)^4}{\sqrt w}\,\rmd\lambda,
\end{equation}
which is~\eref{eq:asym4} with $G$ given by~\eref{eq:Gflrw} ($T=\eta_x$
for the cone that reaches the big bang). For $a=1$, $w=1$ and the
$\lambda$ integral is $T^2$, as in Minkowski spacetime.
